# New Year Fairytale — visual reference analysis

**Review date:** 17 November 2026  
**Scope:** all 16 original JPG files currently stored in `references/products/new-year/fairytale/`. Each original was opened and visually inspected through the GitHub Connector.  
**Scenario authority:** `NEW_YEAR_STORY_SCENARIO.md` remains the approved product direction. This document interprets the visual references; it does not replace or expand the agreed story.  
**Source collection:** Pinterest board supplied by the owner: [Новогодние приглашения](https://pin.it/3lML4kPnE). Individual pin URLs and original creators have not yet been mapped to individual files.

## 1. Overall visual conclusion

The collection is strongest as a **mood, environment, lighting, and winter-forest reference set**. Its recurring visual language is:

- deep blue, blue-green, and dark teal backgrounds;
- warm amber/gold points of light against cool snow and night tones;
- fir branches used as foreground framing;
- soft bokeh, drifting snow, and glowing particles;
- selective shallow focus, with a brighter path or open area drawing the eye into depth.

This is a useful foundation for the opening forest and the atmosphere around the later invitation scenes. It is not a complete visual kit for the agreed story: the collection does **not** provide direct references for the stump, ribboned envelope, magical invitation sheet, or the precise clearing reveal. Those objects and compositions need a separate design pass.

The best direction is an enchanted, cinematic winter forest with restrained warm lights — not a generic framed New Year greeting card. Keep the centre of the scene spatial and believable so the guest feels drawn into a place, rather than looking at a flat decorative background.

## 3. Visual direction recommended for the first design pass

### Palette and light

- **Base:** deep blue-green / dark teal forest shadows.
- **Secondary:** cold blue and muted icy tones in snow and distant atmosphere.
- **Warm accent:** amber / soft gold light sources, small and selective.
- **Snow:** natural cool white, with detail retained rather than pure flat white.
- **Avoid as dominant treatment:** bright turquoise ornamental frames, saturated orange/cyan, or an all-over gold sparkle layer.

The contrast between cool forest depth and warm lights is the most consistent and useful visual signature in the references.

### Composition and depth

Build the forest as a sequence of distinguishable planes:

1. near-camera fir branches, dark and detailed;
2. middle-ground branches and trunks, partially lit;
3. distant trees and a receding snowy route;
4. a clearing that becomes visible only after the foreground/middle planes part;
5. restrained snow and light particles at different apparent depths.

Use a vertical-first composition for the guest's mobile screen, while preserving enough width for desktop. The wide reference EFM-000001AD can inform the landscape logic, but it should not dictate the mobile crop.

### Animation implications

The still references suggest useful motion cues, but they do not themselves provide separated animation layers. For a convincing result:

- move foreground branches and middle-ground trees at different speeds;
- use a subtle forward camera drift during the approach;
- let snow and a few light particles move independently at different depths;
- reveal the clearing by moving/removing tree planes, not by simply crossfading to a new unrelated background;
- keep glow animation restrained and avoid constant high-frequency flicker.

If the final scene uses cut-outs from reference images, first verify source rights. Otherwise recreate the forest elements as original assets inspired by the visual language. Do not assume that a flattened Pinterest image can be safely or cleanly separated into animation layers.

## 5. What the collection does not establish

The following details remain design tasks, not approved visual facts:

- the shape, scale, material and placement of the clearing's stump;
- the envelope silhouette, paper, ribbon material, knot and opening mechanics;
- the invitation sheet shape, paper texture, typography and text layout;
- the exact camera path and number of forest planes;
- the precise transition between forest and clearing;
- the form of the flash and the second information sheet.

Do not infer any of these from the snowflake, ornament frame, lanterns or star decoration.

## 6. Recommended next design sequence

1. Create one forest keyframe: mobile portrait composition with foreground, middle and distant planes.
2. Create a second keyframe showing the same forest with the planes parted and the clearing revealed.
3. Design the clearing as a persistent environment, including the stump position and safe text/control areas.
4. Design the envelope and ribbon as a separate hero object; test closed, opening and open states.
5. Design the invitation sheet and its text-safe area; validate the longest expected invitation copy.
6. Design RESPONSE, DECLINED and PARTICIPANT_INFO as state changes within the same visual environment, following the existing scenario.
7. Only after the keyframes are approved, prepare animation assets and implementation notes.

This sequence preserves the agreed narrative and reduces the risk of polishing decorative details before the main spatial composition works.
